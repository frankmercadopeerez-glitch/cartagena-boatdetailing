Stop the Engine If You Notice:

  • A temperature or oil-pressure alarm.
  • Smoke, fuel odor or sudden power loss.
  • Metallic noise, strong vibration or a visible leak.
  • Repeated starting difficulty.

Continuing to operate may turn a localized fault into a larger repair. Follow the manufacturer’s safety guidance and seek qualified assistance.

Frequently Asked Questions

Marine Mechanics in Cartagena

What does a marine engine diagnostic visit include?
We review the reported symptom, fluid levels and visible leaks, fuel, lubrication, cooling, intake, exhaust, belts, hoses, starting and charging. When supported by the engine, we may also read operating parameters or fault codes. We then present the findings and recommended scope before repairs begin.
Do you provide preventive maintenance for yacht engines?
Yes. The scope follows engine hours, service history, observed condition and the manufacturer’s maintenance schedule. It may include oil and filters, impeller, belts, anodes, coolant, fuel-system items, clamps, hoses, leak checks and operating tests.
Do you assess inboard engines, outboards and marine generators?
We assess inboard engines, outboards and marine generators. Service feasibility, parts, tools, timing and access are confirmed from the make, model, serial number, installation and initial diagnosis.
When should I stop the engine and request an inspection?
Stop the engine if a temperature or oil-pressure alarm appears, or if you notice unusual smoke, sudden power loss, strong vibration, fuel odor, a visible fluid leak, metallic noise or repeated starting difficulty. Follow the engine manufacturer’s safety instructions and seek qualified assistance.
Do you provide a written repair scope before starting?
Yes. After the initial diagnosis, we define labor, parts, required tests and estimated timing in writing. If disassembly reveals additional damage, we document it and request authorization before expanding the work.
