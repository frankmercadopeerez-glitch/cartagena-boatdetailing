Request an Inspection If You Notice:

  • Hot cables, burning odor or repeated breaker trips.
  • Unexpected battery discharge or charging alarms.
  • A/C high-pressure alarms, loss of seawater flow or poor cooling.
  • Corroded terminals, intermittent equipment or visible insulation damage.

Isolate affected equipment only when it is safe to do so, follow the manufacturer’s instructions and seek qualified assistance.

Frequently Asked Questions

Marine Electrical and Yacht A/C

Do you install new marine air-conditioning systems on yachts?
Yes. We first assess cabin volume, available electrical capacity, ventilation, duct routes, seawater intake and discharge, condensate drainage and access. The selected unit, pump, strainer, controls, protection devices and commissioning tests are defined in the written scope.
What marine electrical work do you handle?
We assess and quote wiring, distribution panels, batteries and charging, cabin and navigation lighting, pumps, controls, solar charging and related 12 V, 24 V or AC circuits. Feasibility depends on the existing system, access, equipment compatibility and inspection findings.
How long does a marine electrical or A/C project take?
Timing depends on access, fault-finding, vessel layout, cable routes, equipment availability and the approved scope. We provide an estimated schedule after inspection and update it if hidden conditions or additional authorized work are found.
Do you install solar charging systems on yachts?
Yes. We can assess rigid or semiflexible marine panels, charge controllers, batteries, protection devices and cable routing. System size and expected output are calculated from the vessel's energy use, available area, shading and existing charging system.
What coverage applies to marine electrical installations?
Any workmanship or equipment coverage is stated in the written quote according to the selected components, approved scope and pre-existing system condition. The handover identifies installed materials, protection devices and completed tests.
