Multi-Stage Gelcoat Correction

In Cartagena this service is often called polichar, but it is far more than applying a quick polish. It is a technical process requiring marine-paint knowledge, professional rotary and dual-action polishers, and compounds selected for the exact condition of the gelcoat.

  • Oxidation Removal

    We remove the chalky, dull layer caused by Cartagena's tropical climate.

  • Scratch Correction

    We level light and medium scratches with controlled compounding.

  • Final Mirror Finish

    A showroom finish with strong reflection and restored color depth.

Deep 3-Stage Polishing

We perform a single type of polishing: deep and complete in 3 stages (cut, refine and finish). We remove oxidation, scratches, water spots and swirl marks to restore the mirror gloss of the gelcoat. No intermediate levels or half-done jobs: every vessel gets the full deep correction.

Professional Polishing Process

Technical note: Polishing is a controlled correction process. We first inspect the surface and select the least aggressive method capable of achieving the agreed result.

1

Initial Condition Assessment

We assess oxidation, scratch depth, swirl marks, previous repairs and severely damaged areas. This inspection defines the viable correction level and whether polishing can deliver the expected finish.

2

Decontamination and Preparation

We remove salt, dirt, grease and embedded residue. When the condition calls for it, localized sanding is included in the written scope. A clean surface helps prevent new marks during correction.

3

Stage 1: Compounding

A cutting compound and the appropriate machine and pad are selected for the gelcoat condition to reduce oxidation and correct defects without forcing the surface.

4

Stage 2: Refining Polish

We refine compounding marks and holograms with a medium or fine polish, restoring clarity and color depth.

5

Stage 3: Finishing Polish

A fine finishing polish and soft pad produce the final gloss agreed for the surface, followed by a visual quality check.

6

Optional Ceramic Protection

After correction, ceramic protection can be quoted as an additional service. Product, preparation, maintenance and applicable coverage are specified in the written proposal.

Conditions We Correct

Gelcoat Oxidation

Constant UV exposure in Cartagena can leave gelcoat chalky, dull and faded. Controlled correction removes the oxidized surface layer when the substrate is still suitable for polishing.

Swirl Marks and Holograms

Fine circular marks caused by improper washing or earlier machine polishing are refined with the correct pad, compound and cross-pass technique.

Water Spots

Mineral deposits can etch the surface when water dries in the sun. We select chemical decontamination or mechanical correction according to the depth of the marks.

Surface Scratches

Marks from ropes, fenders, docks and improper washing can often be reduced when they remain within the polishable surface. Deeper damage requires a separate repair.

Color-Matched Gelcoat Scratch Repair

As an additional service, we fill deep scratches with gelcoat and prepare the tone to match the vessel's current color as closely as possible. The repaired area is then leveled and polished into the surrounding finish.

This work is charged per scratch and quoted after inspecting the boat. Price depends on depth, length, location, access and the difficulty of reproducing the aged color.

Frequently Asked Questions

What You Need to Know

How often should gelcoat be polished in Cartagena?
The right interval depends on the gelcoat condition, exposure to sun and salt water, use, and any existing protection. We inspect the surface and recommend a maintenance plan suited to the vessel.
Does professional polishing wear down or damage gelcoat?
Polishing removes a controlled amount of the surface layer. We first assess the gelcoat condition and choose a correction level that does not force a surface that is too thin, burned or severely deteriorated.
What is the difference between sanding and polishing gelcoat?
Sanding uses abrasive paper to remove a thicker layer of deteriorated gelcoat. Polishing uses liquid compounds for controlled correction of surface oxidation and fine scratches. Sanding may be needed first for deeper defects; polishing provides the final refinement.
Can polishing remove deep hull scratches?
Fine and superficial scratches can often be corrected through compounding, polishing and finishing. Deep scratches require a separate color-matched gelcoat repair. This work is priced per scratch after the boat is inspected.
How long does polishing a 40-foot yacht take?
The schedule is confirmed after we inspect the vessel size, access and actual gelcoat condition. Oxidation, scratches, previous repairs and the need to protect sensitive areas can change the work time.
What happens when gelcoat is left unpolished for years?
Neglected gelcoat can lose gloss, become porous and discolored, and eventually crack or peel. If the surface is too deteriorated for safe correction, we may recommend a full paint system instead of polishing.
