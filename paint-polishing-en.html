Why Gelcoat Polishing in the Caribbean?

Cartagena's tropical sun, high UV index and salt air attack unprotected gelcoat year-round. A vessel left without polishing for 2+ years in the Caribbean will develop heavy oxidation, chalking and micro-cracks that permanently reduce resale value.

Our multi-stage correction process recovers the original depth of shine and, when followed by ceramic coating or PPF, can extend the result by 2–5 years. We measure gelcoat thickness before each job — never removing more than necessary.

Frequently Asked Questions

How often should gelcoat be polished in Cartagena?

For vessels without ceramic or PPF protection, once a year — ideally before the December high season. With ceramic coating, deep polishing can be extended to every 2–3 years.

Does professional polishing damage the gelcoat?

Polishing removes 1–5 microns per session. New gelcoat is 400–600 microns thick, so annual wear is minimal. We measure gelcoat thickness before every job to prevent over-cutting.

Can polishing remove deep scratches?

Micro-scratches and surface marks are removed with cutting compound + polish + buffing. Deep scratches reaching the fiberglass require gelcoat repair before the general polishing job.

How long does polishing a 40-ft yacht take?

For a 40-ft yacht in normal maintenance condition: 2–3 days. Heavily oxidized gelcoat requiring intensive correction can extend to 4 days.

What happens if gelcoat is not polished for years?

Neglected gelcoat in the Caribbean loses shine, becomes porous and yellows, and eventually the resin cracks and flakes. At that stage, full gelcoat restoration costs 3–10x more than preventive annual polishing.
